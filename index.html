What we claim for

The disrepair issues your landlord must fix

If any of these issues have been ignored, delayed or badly repaired, your landlord is legally responsible and may owe you substantial compensation.

Damp & Mould

Black toxic mould, persistent condensation, wet patches, peeling wallpaper, and crumbling plaster — including serious respiratory impacts like asthma, bronchitis, and chest infections.

Structural Cracks

Deep cracks in interior walls, ceilings or foundations, sagging floors, unstable staircases, or external brickwork failure that compromises your safety.

Infestations

Rats, mice, cockroaches, silverfish or bedbugs gaining entry through unsealed holes, defective pipes, or untreated structural decay.

Leaks & Water Ingress

Persistent water leaks from upstairs flats, roofing failures, burst internal pipework, and water dripping through light fittings causing electrical hazards.

Boiler & Heating Failure

Broken boilers, intermittent or zero hot water, faulty radiators, and freezing living conditions leaving vulnerable families in dangerous cold.

Rotten Windows & Doors

Rotten wooden frames, broken locks, draughty single glazing that cannot retain warmth, or doors and windows that will not open or close securely.

Statutory Protection & Official Legal Sources

Your Legal Rights as a UK Social Housing Tenant

Under United Kingdom legislation, council and housing association landlords have strict statutory repair duties. If they neglect these duties, the law is firmly on your side.

Landlord & Tenant Act 1985
Legislation.gov.uk · Section 11

The cornerstone UK law obligating social landlords to keep the structure, exterior, heating, water, gas, sanitation, and electrical installations in full repair.

View Statutory Legislation →
Homes Fitness Act & Awaab's Law
GOV.UK Official Guidance

Mandates that all rented social homes must be fit for human habitation from day one, setting legal strict timelines for councils to eradicate hazardous damp and mould.

Read GOV.UK Guidance →
The Housing Ombudsman
Official Dispute Service

Independent national body that investigates severe complaints and landlord maladministration across all registered social housing providers and councils in England.

Who can make a housing disrepair claim in the UK?

Any tenant of a UK local council or housing association whose landlord has failed to fix serious disrepair after being informed. Private tenants are not covered by this service.

How much compensation could I get?

Most successful claims range from £1,500 to £15,000 depending on the severity of the disrepair, how long it lasted, health impacts, and any belongings damaged.

Does it really cost nothing?

Yes. Our partner solicitors work on a strict No Win, No Fee basis. If your claim isn't successful, you pay absolutely nothing.

Do I need to have reported the damage already?

It helps, but it's not always required. If you haven't reported it in writing yet, our solicitors will advise you on the best next step during your free case review.

Will making a claim get me evicted?

No. It is illegal for a council or housing association to evict you for making a legitimate disrepair claim. This is called 'retaliatory eviction' and is protected against under UK law.

How long does a claim take?

Most claims settle within 6 to 12 months, though urgent repairs can often be secured much faster once solicitors serve a formal letter of claim on the landlord.
